The problem: applying in the dark

Who has it

People who have been out of the labor market, who have never entered it, or who are just starting out.

How they handle it today

They apply ad nauseam. Applicant tracking systems screen many of them out, and there is very little guidance on which jobs to apply for.

What it costs them

Time: hours of applications without knowing how well they match.
Frustration: when no one calls back, the search takes a real psychological toll.

What we cut, measured

  • Remember every decision: a repeat search went from about 45,000 tokens and 94 seconds to 0 model calls and 2 seconds
  • Think less where it is safe: the shortlist went from about 43 seconds to 9
  • Warming the prompt cache saved nothing, so we removed it
  • Since the OpenAI key, the provider caches repeated input automatically: about 37% cached, a run about 9% cheaper

Safety: attacks tested, defenses built

  • 8 of 8 safety tests pass. The first run failed 3, including bias: adding age, gender, religion and disability lowered her chance of being hired from 55% to 38%. Those traits are now removed before any coach sees the profile.
  • Red team, 15 attacks: 12 of 15 stopped before the new defenses, 15 of 15 after, and all four booby traps caught their attacker.

Impact: a shorter road to a job

One real run: 151 jobs down to the 4 where she had at least a 50% chance of an interview and an offer.

At The Chart Academy

One case manager serves about 40 to 70 students a year. ForeSIGHT gives each student a head start: a profile from their own words, the skills they undersell, and a short list of jobs worth applying for. 21% of placed alumni were starting their first job.

At scale (scenario)

One organization like ours in 30 cities: 1,200 to 2,100 job seekers a year for about $567 a year in model cost, roughly a third of what WIOA spends on career services for one participant ($1,600).
